Cellular Transport
1. Passive Transport
         -Diffusion of substance across a membrane with no energy investment
         -Move from high to low concentration (concentration gradient)
    Examples:
         a. Diffusion – tendency for molecules of any substance to spread out evenly into the available
space.
         b. Facilitated Diffusion – aided by transport proteins (speed the movement of molecules across
the plasma membrane)
         c. Osmosis - movement of water across a semipermeable membrane (diffusion of water)
                      - affected by the concentration gradient of dissolved substance (solution’s tonicity).
         c.1. Hypotonic solution - concentration of solute is lower than inside the cell
                                   - water comes inside the cell
                                   - clues: turgid, swell, burst, lysed (Hypotonic- pumutok si cell)
         c.2. Isotonic solution - concentration of solute outside is the same with inside the cell
                                - NO NET movement of water
         c.3. Hypertonic solution - concentration of solute outside is greater than inside the cell.
                                    - water goes outside the cell
                                    - clues: plasmolysis, shrink, shrivel, dehydrated
2. Active Transport
         -Uses energy to move solutes against their concentration gradients
         -Requires energy, usually in the form of ATP
         -Examples: Ion pumps, cotransport, bulk transport (exocytosis, endocytosis)
3. Bulk Transport
         -transport of large molecules
         -phagocytosis (cell eating)
         -pinocytosis (cell drinking)

Theory of Evolution
(Jean Baptiste de Lamarck)
-Theory of Need
            • Production of new organism arises from the need of it
-Theory of Use and Disuse
            • Continued use of an organ strengthens it and continued disuse weakens it until it
                disappears
-Theory of Inheritance of Acquired Characteristics
            • Characteristics that have been acquired be change during the lifetime of the organisms
                is transmitted to the offspring
Theory of Natural Selection
            • Charles Darwin
            • Differential survival and reproduction of individuals due to differences in phenotype.
            • “Survival of the Fittest” (Spencer)

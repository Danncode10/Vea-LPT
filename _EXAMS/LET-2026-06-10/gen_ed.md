When Typhoon Yolanda struck the central Visayas in November 2013, it became one of the most powerful tropical cyclones ever recorded at landfall. The storm killed over 6,300 people, displaced more than four million, and obliterated entire coastal towns through a storm surge that reached six meters in Tacloban. International aid poured in within days, but recovery on the ground revealed a deeper problem: many residents had not anticipated a "storm surge" because the term — borrowed from English meteorological vocabulary — had not been clearly translated into local advisories. PAGASA later introduced more concrete warnings such as "*delubyo*" and visual height indicators in Filipino and Waray. The Yolanda experience reshaped Philippine disaster-risk communication and underscored how lives can hinge not only on data but on the language used to convey it.

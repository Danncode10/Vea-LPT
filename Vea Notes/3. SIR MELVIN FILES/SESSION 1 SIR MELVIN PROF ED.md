RA 4670 – MAGNA CARTA FOR PUBLIC SCHOOL TEACHERS

Purpose of the Law
	•	To promote the welfare and protection of public school teachers
	•	To improve their working conditions and professional growth
	•	To ensure teachers can perform their duties without undue pressure or exploitation

Key Provisions (Simplified)
1. Recruitment & Qualifications
	•	Teachers must meet proper educational qualifications
	•	Hiring must be based on merit and fitness, not favoritism
2. Salary & Compensation
	•	Teachers should receive fair and competitive salaries
	•	Salary scale must reflect professional status
	•	Includes allowances and benefits
3. Working Hours
	•	Standard teaching load: no more than 6 hours of actual classroom teaching per day
	•	Excess workload must be properly compensated
4. Teaching Load Protection
	•	Teachers should not be assigned non-teaching tasks excessively
	•	Administrative work should not interfere with teaching quality
5. Health & Safety Benefits
	•	Access to medical care and insurance benefits
	•	Protection from unsafe working conditions
6. Academic Freedom
	•	Teachers are given freedom in teaching methods
	•	Protection from interference in professional judgment
7. Professional Growth
	•	Right to trainings, seminars, and scholarships
	•	Encouragement for continuous development
8. Security of Tenure
	•	Teachers cannot be dismissed without due process
	•	Protection from arbitrary removal

BENEFITS OF TEACHERS ACCORDING TO THE RA 4670
Provision / Benefit
What the Magna Carta Really Says
Key Details
1. Married Teachers – Locality
When possible, the government should assign married teachers in the same locality or municipality.
Helps keep families together. Often appears in LET as “assignment of husband and wife teachers.”
2. Academic Freedom
Teachers enjoy academic freedom in the discharge of their professional duties.
Means freedom in methods and strategies of teaching as long as aligned with curriculum and policies. NOT absolute freedom.
3. Non-Discrimination
No discrimination in entrance to the teaching profession or during employment.
Cannot discriminate based on sex, civil status, religion, political affiliation, etc. Merit and fitness should prevail.
4. Freedom of Association
Teachers have the right to organize and join organizations or unions.
Includes joining professional organizations and teacher associations for protection of rights and welfare.
5. Salary Scale
Teachers should receive salaries comparable with other professions requiring similar qualifications.
Salary progression is through salary steps and promotions. The “every 3 years increase” refers to step increments based on existing government compensation rules, not directly stated exactly that way in RA 4670.
6. Cost of Living Allowance (COLA)
Teachers are entitled to cost-of-living allowances authorized by law.
Given to help teachers cope with inflation and rising prices of goods.
7. Special Hardship Allowance
Teachers assigned in hardship posts receive additional compensation.
At least 25% of basic pay for those in difficult, remote, or hazardous areas. Common LET phrase: “far-flung areas.”
8. Teaching Hours
Actual classroom teaching shall not exceed 6 hours a day.
Teachers still complete the normal 8-hour workday. Excess teaching requires additional compensation. Remaining hours are for preparation, checking, records, and other school duties.
9. Leave Privileges
Teachers enjoy vacation and sick leave privileges.
Study Leave: teachers who rendered at least 7 years of service may receive study leave of up to 1 school year with at least 60% of monthly salary. Usually for graduate studies.
10. Salary Increase Upon Retirement
Retirement benefits should reflect salary increases granted by law before retirement.
Connected with retirement laws and computation of pension/benefits.
11. Medical Examination and Treatment
Free medical examination for teachers.
Physical examination should be provided once a year according to the law, not necessarily twice a year. If illness is due to work, treatment expenses may be covered by the government.

	•	Imperfect right

Right Type
Core Definition
Simple Explanation
Concrete Example
Inalienable Right
Rights inherent to human dignity that cannot be surrendered, transferred, or taken away.
You are born with it, and you cannot legally give it up even if you want to.
The right to life, personal safety, and protection from abuse.
Alienable Right
Rights that can be legally transferred, sold, given away, or surrendered to another party.
You own it, but you can choose to give it or sell it to someone else.
The right to own private property, land, or a specific piece of money.
Perfect Right
Rights that are strictly defined and enforceable by state laws, creating a legal duty for others.
A right backed by clear laws; you can sue or go to court if someone violates it.
The right to receive a salary under an employment contract.
Imperfect Right
Rights based on morals, ethics, or religion that cannot be legally enforced in court.
Something you should receive out of kindness, but no law forces people to give it.
The right of a poor person to receive charity or pity from others.

1. Which ethical framework emphasizes duty and moral rules?
	•	Utilitarianism
	•	Deontology
	•	Relativism
	•	Hedonism

Utilitarianism
	•	Proponents: Jeremy Bentham and John Stuart Mill 
	•	Main Idea: “Greatest good for the greatest number.” 
	•	Morality depends on the consequences of an action. 
	•	An action is right if it produces the most happiness or benefit for many people.
	•	Example: Sacrificing one life to save many people may be considered acceptable.

Deontology
	•	Proponent: Immanuel Kant 
	•	Main Idea: Duty and moral law. 
	•	An action is right if it follows moral rules and obligations, even if the outcome is bad. 
	•	Ethics should be based on principles, not consequences. 
	•	Example: Telling the truth because it is morally right, even if it causes trouble. Deontology emphasizes duty, obligation, and universal moral rules

Relativism
	•	Associated Thinkers: Franz Boas and Ruth Benedict 
	•	Main Idea: Morality depends on culture, society, or personal beliefs. 
	•	There is no universal right or wrong. 
	•	Example: A practice may be acceptable in one culture but wrong in another.

Hedonism
	•	Proponent: Epicurus 
	•	Main Idea: Pleasure is the highest good. 
	•	People should seek pleasure and avoid pain. 
	•	Example: Choosing actions that bring happiness and enjoyment.

11. Who proposed the “categorical imperative”?
	•	John Stuart Mill
	•	Confucius
	•	Socrates
	•	Immanuel Kant

What is the Categorical Imperative?
It is Kant’s rule that: A person should act only according to principles that could become universal laws.

Simple Meaning:
Before doing something, ask: “What if everyone did this?” If the action would create a good and moral society when universally applied, then it is morally right.

Example:
Lying is wrong because if everyone lied, trust would disappear.

14. Which disease is characterized by progressive memory loss and cognitive decline, primarily affecting older adults?
	•	Parkinson's disease
	•	Epilepsy
	•	Alzheimer's disease
	•	Multiple sclerosis

Parkinson’s disease
	•	Mainly affects movement and motor control, not primarily memory. 
	•	Symptoms include tremors, stiffness, slow movement, and balance problems. 
	•	Some cognitive decline may occur later, but it is not the defining feature. 

Epilepsy
	•	Characterized by recurrent seizures due to abnormal brain activity. 
	•	Memory loss is not the main progressive feature of this condition. 

Alzheimer’s disease
	•	The most common cause of dementia in older adults. 
	•	Characterized by: 
	•	Progressive memory loss 
	•	Confusion 
	•	Difficulty thinking and reasoning 
	•	Decline in daily functioning 

Multiple sclerosis
	•	Affects the central nervous system, leading to problems with movement, sensation, and coordination. 
	•	Cognitive issues may occur but are not the primary symptom.

23. Which process involves reflecting on significant life experiences to determine what is important to an individual?
	•	Goal setting
	•	Skill acquisition
	•	Habit formation
	•	Values clarification

Goal setting
	•	Meaning: Identifying specific objectives you want to achieve in the future. 
	•	Focuses on what you want to accomplish, not on reflecting on life experiences. 

Skill acquisition
	•	Meaning: Learning and developing abilities or competencies. 
	•	Example: learning to teach, cook, or compute. 

Habit formation
	•	Meaning: Developing repeated behaviors through practice. 
	•	Example: studying daily, waking up early. 

Values clarification
	•	Meaning: A process of reflecting on life experiences to understand what is truly important to a person. 
	•	Helps individuals identify their core beliefs, priorities, and guiding principles. 
	•	Example:A person reflects on experiences of helping others and realizes that service and compassion are important values.

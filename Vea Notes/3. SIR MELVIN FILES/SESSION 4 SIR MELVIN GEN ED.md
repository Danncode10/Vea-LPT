Term / Concept
Definition
Two Seasons Only
The Philippines has a tropical and maritime climate with only two seasons: Wet and Dry. It does not have winter, spring, or autumn.
Amihan (Northeast Monsoon)
Cool, dry air blowing from Siberia and China; active from December to February; brings cool breezes and Christmas weather.
Habagat (Southwest Monsoon)
Warm, moist air blowing from the equator; active from June to November; brings heavy rains and frequent typhoons.
Hot Dry Season
The local Philippine summer; active from March to May; characterized by peak heat and the highest temperatures when monsoons weaken.
Meteorology
The scientific study of the atmosphere and weather forecasting; led locally by the government agency PAGASA.

TYPES OF TAX SYSTEM






















	•	Progressive (Curving Up): As a person's income increases, their tax rate also goes up. The graph curves upward because high earners are placed in higher tax brackets.

	•	Proportional (Flat Line): The tax rate stays exactly the same for everyone, whether income is low, middle, or high. It is a straight, flat horizontal line.

	•	Regressive (Curving Down): As a person's income increases, the tax takes a smaller percentage of their total income. It curves downward because it hurts low-income earners the most.

	•	Constitutional vs. Statutory: Constitutional rights cannot be changed easily by ordinary congressmen. Statutory rights can be updated, modified, or completely removed by a new law passed in Congress (e.g., changing the legal retirement age).

	•	Inalienable vs. Alienable: Your right to life is inalienable—you cannot legally sign it away or sell yourself into slavery. However, your right to sell a piece of land or transfer a car title is an alienable property right.

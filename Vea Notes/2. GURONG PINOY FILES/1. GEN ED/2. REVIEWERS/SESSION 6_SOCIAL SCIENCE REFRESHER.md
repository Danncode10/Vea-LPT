	•	Rizal

MERCADO: Market

RIZAL: Green field
The surname "Rizal" is derived from the Spanish word ricial, which means a "green field," "second crop," or "pasture," representing a "green field ready for harvest". It was adopted by the family of Philippine national hero Jose Rizal, who was originally named Mercado, to avoid Spanish suspicion and represent their agricultural roots.

CLAVERIA DECREE: change surname
The Rizal family adopted "Rizal" as a secondary surname due to the Clavería Decree of 1849, a Spanish colonial law mandating Filipinos to adopt new, standardized surnames from a provided catalog to improve census records and tax collection.

TITLE: NOLI ME TANGERE
	•	Literal Meaning: The title Noli Me Tángere is a Latin phrase that translates to "TOUCH ME NOT" or "DO NOT TOUCH ME".
	•	Biblical Source: It originates from the Gospel of JOHN (20:17), where Jesus speaks these words to Mary Magdalene after His resurrection.
	•	Medical Metaphor: José Rizal, who trained as an ophthalmologist, used the phrase as a 19th-century medical term for an excruciatingly painful cancer of the eye or skin that was too sensitive to be touched.
	•	Social & Symbolic Meaning: Rizal used this medical concept to represent the "SOCIAL CANCER" infecting Philippine society under Spanish rule. It signified systemic corruptions—such as abuses by Spanish friars and the colonial government—that were so deeply rooted, sensitive, and taboo that people refused to speak of or "touch" them. CORE THEMES OF THE NOVEL
	•	Clerical Abuses & Hypocrisy: Exposure of the corruption, greed, and moral failings of Spanish friars (personified by characters like Padre Dámaso and Padre Salví) who used religion to exploit and control the local population.
	•	Education as Empowerment: Reinforcing the concept that enlightenment and education, rather than violent rebellion, are the primary tools for freeing a society from oppression.
	•	Colonial Mentality: Satirizing the desperation of certain elite Filipinos (like Doña Victorina) who abandoned their native heritage to frantically mimic Spanish customs.
	•	National Consciousness: Awakening a collective Filipino identity and sense of shared homeland out of a fragmented and oppressed colony.

TITLE: EL FILIBUSTERISMO
	•	Literal Meaning: The title EL FILIBUSTERISMO translates from Spanish to "The Subversion", "The Filibustering", or "THE REIGN OF GREED".
	•	Historical Definition: In the 19th century, a filibustero in the Philippines was a subversive, a political agitator, or a rebel who worked to disrupt Spanish colonial rule. It was a dangerous label used by authorities to mark reform-minded Filipinos for execution or exile.
	•	Dedicatory Meaning: José Rizal dedicated the novel to the memory of the GOMBURZA (FATHERS GOMEZ, BURGOS, AND ZAMORA), three Filipino priests falsely accused of mutiny and publicly executed by garrote in 1872.
	•	Symbolic Meaning: While Noli Me Tangere diagnosed the "social cancer," El Filibusterismo represents the desperate, violent reaction to that untreated disease. It symbolizes anger, disillusionment, and the terrifying temptation of bloody revolution when peaceful reform fails.

CORE THEMES OF THE NOVEL
	•	The Failure of Peaceful Reform: The protagonist Simoun (a disguised, cynical Crisostomo Ibarra) abandons his old ideals of education and legal reform, turning instead to radical destruction.
	•	The Ethics of Revolution: The novel serves as a deep philosophical debate on whether a corrupt system can be justly overthrown through violence, ultimately warning that hate and blood only breed more monsters.
	•	Systemic Corruption and Greed: An intense critique of the absolute decay within the colonial government, the military, and the church hierarchy, all driven purely by wealth and power.
	•	Youth and the Future: Highlighted in the famous final monologue by Padre Florentino, the novel challenges the Filipino youth to make themselves worthy of freedom through purity, sacrifice, and moral integrity.

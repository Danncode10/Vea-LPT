Material Culture
Refers to the physical objects, resources, and spaces that people use to define their culture. These include homes, neighborhoods, cities, schools, churches, synagogues, temples, mosques, offices, factories and plants, tools, means of production, goods and products, stores, and so forth. 


TYPES OF NORMS
Folkways
weak norms; informal expectations of behavior. generally, standards for politeness or manners. penalties for violating folkways are minimal and usually enforced by peer-pressure or social pressure.

Mores
strong norms that are considered vitally important and necessary. mores are issues of right and wrong and may be written down as laws. violating mores results in strong disapproval.

Taboos
a more so serious that it is considered unspeakable or disgusting.

Laws
a norm that is written down and enforced by law.

Values
a person's principles or standards of behavior; one's judgment of what is important in life.

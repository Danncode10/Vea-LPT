                                Prayer Before Class Starts
We thank you for this new day, Lord.
Help us to embrace all that it may hold for us.
To hear the birdsong and have new melodies in our hearts.
To see the sunrise and create beauty with our hands.
To touch the leaves that grow afresh,
And allow your love to touch our hearts.
To smell the fragrance of the flowers
And breathe in the wonder of your creation.
To dwell on the beauty of our world,
And study and fathom your miracles.
To enjoy the conversations we have and the lessons we go to,
May we drink in all the goodness you have for us.

• A run-on occurs when what could be two complete sentences -
  that is, two independent clauses - are connected in one sentence
  without being punctuated.
  • Clark had vanished he left his eyeglasses and coat in the telephone
    booth.
• You can add a comma and a coordinating conjunction. FANBOYS -
  that's for, and, nor, but, or, yet, and so. So:
  • Clark had vanished, but he left his eyeglasses and coat in the
    telephone booth.
• You can make the run-on into two separate sentences:
  • Clark had vanished. He left his eyeglasses and coat in the telephone
    booth.
• Or you can add a semicolon:
  • Clark had vanished; however, he left his eyeglasses and coat in the
    telephone booth.

• Whole numbers are all counting numbers, as well as zero.
• Integers are all whole numbers (always remember whole numbers
  include zero), but both the negative and positive versions of each
  whole number.
• Real numbers - numbers that can be identified on a number line
  and can be used to measure distance. Real numbers include
  10,000, 3/4, 0, −365, 980, 125, √51, and 0.3
• Rational numbers are all numbers that can be written as a ratio or
  fraction of two integers, except the denominator can not be zero.
  Rational numbers can also be written as decimals.
Irrational numbers are those numbers that can not fit into the category of rational
numbers. This means that irrational numbers are non-terminating decimals, non-
repeating decimals, square roots that do not simplify, and infinite numbers like π.

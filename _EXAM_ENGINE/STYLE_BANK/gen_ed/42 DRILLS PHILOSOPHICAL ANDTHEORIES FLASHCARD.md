--- Slide 1 ---
Which philosophy emphasizes the spiritual, ideal, and eternal values?

--- Slide 2 ---
➤ IdealismRationale: Idealism asserts that reality is mentally constructed; values, ideas, and the spiritual realm are more fundamental than the physical world.

--- Slide 3 ---
Which philosophy is grounded in observable facts and scientific methods?

--- Slide 4 ---
➤ RealismRationale: Realism believes that reality exists independently of the mind, and knowledge should be based on science and reason.

--- Slide 5 ---
Which philosophy teaches that knowledge comes from sensory experience?

--- Slide 6 ---
➤ EmpiricismRationale: Empiricism emphasizes learning through experience, observation, and evidence collected by the senses.

--- Slide 7 ---
Which philosophy believes development is shaped by nature and innate tendencies?

--- Slide 8 ---
➤ NaturalismRationale: Naturalism upholds that education should follow the natural development of the child, highlighting innate capacities.

--- Slide 9 ---
Which philosophy stresses personal choice, responsibility, and individuality?

--- Slide 10 ---
➤ ExistentialismRationale: Existentialism views humans as free agents responsible for their own development and meaning in life.

--- Slide 11 ---
Which philosophy emphasizes mastery of essential academic subjects?

--- Slide 12 ---
➤ EssentialismRationale: Essentialism promotes a back-to-basics curriculum focusing on foundational skills and knowledge.

--- Slide 13 ---
Which philosophy centers on timeless knowledge and classical literature?

--- Slide 14 ---
➤ PerennialismRationale: Perennialism values enduring truths and teaches ideas from great works of Western civilization.

--- Slide 15 ---
Which philosophy promotes practical application of knowledge through experience?

--- Slide 16 ---
➤ PragmatismRationale: Pragmatism holds that truth is what works; learning occurs through problem-solving and active experimentation.

--- Slide 17 ---
Which philosophy values student interest and experiential learning?

--- Slide 18 ---
➤ ProgressivismRationale: Progressivism is a child-centered approach that emphasizes learning by doing and real-world relevance.

--- Slide 19 ---
Which philosophy advocates moderate pleasure as the path to happiness?

--- Slide 20 ---
➤ EpicureanismRationale: Epicureanism teaches that the goal of life is to attain tranquility and avoid pain—not perfection, but balanced pleasure.

--- Slide 21 ---
Which belief holds that the existence of God cannot be known?

--- Slide 22 ---
➤ AgnosticismRationale: Agnosticism asserts that humans cannot have certain knowledge of divine existence, unlike atheism which denies it.

--- Slide 23 ---
Which philosophy teaches emotional restraint and acceptance of fate?

--- Slide 24 ---
➤ StoicismRationale: Stoicism advocates rational control over emotions and enduring hardship with virtue and inner peace.

--- Slide 25 ---
Which philosophy considers pleasure as the highest good in life?

--- Slide 26 ---
➤ HedonismRationale: Hedonism teaches that the pursuit of pleasure and avoidance of pain is the most ethical and fulfilling life.

--- Slide 27 ---
Which philosophy emphasizes the dignity and worth of the human being?

--- Slide 28 ---
➤ HumanismRationale: Humanism focuses on personal growth, free will, and the development of human potential.

--- Slide 29 ---
Which theory sees learning as constructing meaning from experience and prior knowledge?

--- Slide 30 ---
➤ ConstructivismRationale: Constructivism states learners actively build knowledge based on what they already know and discover.

--- Slide 31 ---
Which philosophy seeks to reform society by solving current issues through education?

--- Slide 32 ---
➤ ReconstructionismRationale: Reconstructionism promotes critical thinking to address social problems and create a better world.

--- Slide 33 ---
Which philosophy blends faith and reason in interpreting Church teachings?

--- Slide 34 ---
➤ ScholasticismRationale: Scholasticism, rooted in medieval education, uses logic and philosophy to rationalize theological doctrines.

--- Slide 35 ---
Who developed the cognitive development theory focused on stages of thinking?

--- Slide 36 ---
➤ Jean PiagetRationale: Piaget described four stages of cognitive development, from sensorimotor to formal operations, showing how children's thinking evolves.

--- Slide 37 ---
Which learning theory emphasizes discovery and scaffolding from concrete to abstract?

--- Slide 38 ---
➤ Cognitivism (Jerome Bruner)Rationale: Bruner advocated spiral curriculum and learning through active discovery, moving from simple to complex ideas.

--- Slide 39 ---
Which learning theory views behavior as shaped by the environment?

--- Slide 40 ---
➤ Behaviorism (John Watson)Rationale: Behaviorism claims learning is a result of conditioning by external stimuli, observable and measurable.

--- Slide 41 ---
Which theory explains learning via stimulus-response associations in a structured environment?

--- Slide 42 ---
➤ Connectionism (Edward Thorndike)Rationale: Thorndike’s laws of learning (effect, readiness, exercise) highlight how repetition and rewards strengthen learning.

--- Slide 43 ---
Which theory focuses on student-centered learning and self-actualization?

--- Slide 44 ---
➤ Humanism (Carl Rogers)Rationale: Carl Rogers emphasized empathy, personal meaning, and emotional safety in learner-centered environments.

--- Slide 45 ---
Which learning theory uses reinforcement or punishment to shape behavior?

--- Slide 46 ---
➤ Operant Conditioning (B.F. Skinner)Rationale: Skinner showed how positive/negative reinforcement or punishment influences voluntary behavior.

--- Slide 47 ---
Which theory explains learning through pairing of stimuli and responses?

--- Slide 48 ---
➤ Classical Conditioning (Ivan Pavlov)Rationale: Learning occurs when a neutral stimulus becomes associated with a reflexive response.

--- Slide 49 ---
Which theory encourages meaningful learning through concept mapping and prior knowledge?

--- Slide 50 ---
➤ Meaningful Learning (David Ausubel)Rationale: Ausubel emphasized advance organizers and meaningful connection of new material with existing cognitive structures.

--- Slide 51 ---
Which theory involves sudden understanding and restructuring of information?

--- Slide 52 ---
➤ Insightful Learning (Wolfgang Köhler)Rationale: Insight learning happens when the learner grasps the whole problem and arrives at a solution through perception.

--- Slide 53 ---
Who developed the theory of moral development with preconventional to postconventional stages?

--- Slide 54 ---
➤ Lawrence KohlbergRationale: Kohlberg proposed six stages under three levels where moral reasoning develops over time.

--- Slide 55 ---
Who developed the hierarchy of human needs affecting motivation?

--- Slide 56 ---
➤ Abraham MaslowRationale: Maslow’s pyramid shows people must satisfy basic needs before reaching self-actualization.

--- Slide 57 ---
Who proposed that early bonds with caregivers affect lifelong emotional health?

--- Slide 58 ---
➤ John BowlbyRationale: Bowlby’s attachment theory emphasizes secure early relationships for healthy development.

--- Slide 59 ---
Who expanded on Erikson’s identity theory by describing four identity statuses?

--- Slide 60 ---
➤ James MarciaRationale: Marcia identified identity diffusion, foreclosure, moratorium, and achievement during adolescence.

--- Slide 61 ---
Which theory considers the dynamic interaction of individual and environment?

--- Slide 62 ---
➤ Field Theory (Kurt Lewin)Rationale: Lewin’s theory views behavior as a function of both personal and environmental factors (B = f(P,E)).

--- Slide 63 ---
Which theory explains development as influenced by multiple environmental systems?

--- Slide 64 ---
➤ Bioecological Theory (Urie Bronfenbrenner)Rationale: Includes microsystem (family, school), mesosystem, exosystem, macrosystem, and chronosystem.

--- Slide 65 ---
Who proposed that behavior is guided by internal control and responsible choices?

--- Slide 66 ---
➤ Choice Theory (William Glasser)Rationale: Glasser argued that people choose behavior to satisfy basic needs: survival, love, power, freedom, and fun.

--- Slide 67 ---
Who emphasized learning through observation and imitation of models?

--- Slide 68 ---
➤ Albert BanduraRationale: Bandura’s social learning theory highlights the role of modeling, imitation, and vicarious reinforcement.

--- Slide 69 ---
Which theory explains learning as a social process involving guidance from a more skilled individual?

--- Slide 70 ---
➤ Socio-Cultural Theory (Lev Vygotsky)Rationale: Vygotsky stressed the importance of the More Knowledgeable Other (MKO) and scaffolding in learning within the Zone of Proximal Development.

--- Slide 71 ---
What is the term for thinking about one’s own thinking processes?

--- Slide 72 ---
➤ MetacognitionRationale: Metacognition helps learners regulate and evaluate their understanding and strategies.

--- Slide 73 ---
What technique involves verbalizing one's thoughts during learning?

--- Slide 74 ---
➤ RecitationRationale: Speaking aloud helps reinforce learning and clarify concepts.

--- Slide 75 ---
What term refers to formal, structured education controlled by institutions or teachers?

--- Slide 76 ---
➤ SchoolingRationale: Schooling delivers standard instruction within a systematic educational setting.

--- Slide 77 ---
What is a symbolic representation or riddle using images and words to convey ideas?

--- Slide 78 ---
➤ RebusRationale: Rebus puzzles convey messages through a mix of visuals and text, often used for creative learning.

--- Slide 79 ---
What method teaches religious doctrine without room for questioning or change?

--- Slide 80 ---
➤ IndoctrinationRationale: Indoctrination involves imposing beliefs rigidly without critical examination or variation.

--- Slide 81 ---
What does KASH stand for in the context of learner development?

--- Slide 82 ---
➤ Knowledge, Attitudes, Skills, and HabitsRationale: Represents the holistic dimensions of learning essential for effective education.

--- Slide 83 ---
What does KCAASE represent in Bloom’s Taxonomy?

--- Slide 84 ---
➤ Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, EvaluationRationale: Describes a hierarchy of cognitive skills from basic recall to critical thinking and judgment.

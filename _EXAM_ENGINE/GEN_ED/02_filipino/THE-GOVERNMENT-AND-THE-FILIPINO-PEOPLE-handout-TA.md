The Concept of State
A state is a community of people, more or less numerous, permanently occupying a definite portion of territory,

independent of external control and possessing an organized government to which the great body of inhabitants
render habitual

Four Elements of State
1. People
2. Territory
3. Government
4. Sovereignty or independence from external control

1. People/population
e Without people there can be no State.
e The absence of population in Antarctica makes it a non-state.
e There is no specific number of people that should constitute a state; what important is ... People must be
sufficient to provide governing body and a number of persons to be governed, & of course,
sufficient to support a state organization & to fulfill its international organization.

2. Territory
The territory of the State consists of:
1. All the lands and water embraced therein including all lakes and rivers within its boundaries & even
rivers within its boundaries & even lakes & rivers which serve as boundaries.
2. The territorial sea, the air space, the subsoil, the seabed, the insular shelves and other submarine areas
over which the state has jurisdiction or sovereignty.

3. Government

e Government is a form or system of administration by which a community is managed.

4. Sovereignty
It is the inherent, supreme and absolute power of the state to command obedience from all its
subjects and its freedom from external.

e Internal Sovereignty- refers to the power of the ruler or of the people to govern within the state.
e External sovereignty or independence - is the right of the state to pursue its aim and purposes
without interference from other states.

THE CONSTITUTION
Constitution is the fundamental law of the land

- According to Judge Cooley - It is the fundamental law of the state, containing the principles upon which a
government is founded, regulating the division of the sovereign powers and directing to what person each
of these powers is to be exercised.

Constitution maybe classified as:
1. Democratic, autocratic, oligarchic or socialistic, depending on the type of government
2. Written or unwritten — written - if prepared by a Constitutional convention; unwritten- if composed of customs
and political practices
3. 3. Flexible or rigid — flexible if it is easy to change and rigid if a convention is called to amend it.
4. 4. Granted by the ruling monarch or the product of a deliberate act of a sovereign people.

Six Parts of a Written Constitution
1. A Preamble which embodies the ideals of the state
2. A statement of territorial limits or boundaries of the state
3. Bill of rights- a statement of the fundamental civil & political rights of the people which the government pledges
to safeguard
4. A statement of the organization, form and distribution of the powers of government
Provisions for amendatory process-whereby formal changes in the Constitution may be brought about
6. The date of the effectivity of the Constitution

IMPEACHMENT- is a method provided by the Constitution through which high officials of the government with long
and definite terms may be removed before their terms expire.

The officials who can be removed by impeachment are as follows:
1. President
2. Vice President
3. Justices of the Supreme Court
4. Members of the Constitutional Commissions
5. Ombudsman

Grounds for Impeachment:
1. Culpable violation of the Constitution-when an officer knowingly disregards its provisions.

2. Treason- is a crime committed by citizens who extend aid to a government or people with whom their country
is at war.

3. Bribery- is a crime committed by a public official who receives money or gifts from other persons in the
performance of an official function

4. Betrayal of public trust- involves actions which tend to bring the office into dispute. Abuse of authority,
inexcusable negligence of duty and shameful conduct are some examples.

            Collective Nouns as Subjects

A collective noun or group noun identifies a single entity that is
  composed of more than one unit: class, team, faculty, majority, family,
  series, committee, jury, audience, crowd. Collective nouns are singular
  when they act as one unit.

RULE 5: When the subject is a collective noun it should be followed by a
 singular verb (with –s or –es) -- unless the collective noun is plural
 (teams) or the context of the sentence emphasizes each individual in
 the group.



Examples:
- The committee meets Friday at noon.
- A majority is needed to pass the bill.
- Every afternoon the baseball team follows its coach out to the hot
  field for practice.
          Collective Nouns as Subjects

EXCEPTION:

- The committee are busy working on their respective tasks.

- The committees meet each Friday at noon.

- A majority of students feel unsafe on school grounds.

- A number of parents appeal to the Department of Education on the
  possible resumption of face-to-face classes.

- After the three-hour practice under the sun, the team
  shower, change into their street clothes, and go to their air-
  conditioned homes.
          Titles and Phrases as Subjects

RULE 5: When the subject is a title, a specified word or
 phrase, or a gerund phrase, treat it as a single unit and
 follow it with a base form of the verb.


Title: Cats is my favorite musical.

Specified phrase: “Once upon a time” is the standard introduction to a
  fairy tale.

Gerund phrase: Delivering pizzas is not a glamorous job, but it pays
  my bill.
          Singular Plurals as Subjects

Rule 5: Singular plurals (handful of words) are actually
 singular most of the time (e. g., economics, news).
 These are singular unless they describe individual
 entities.


Examples:

- Physics is my favorite subject.

- Statistics shows a strong correlation between goal setting and
  achievement.

- These statistics show a strong correlation between goal setting and
  achievement.

(singular or plural) with the noun to which it refers. Here, the plural pronoun "their"
incorrectly refers to the singular noun "committee.

    The other options contain no errors. In (A), the preposition "After" appropriately
introduces a phrase that indicates when the committee made its decision. In (B), "to
postpone" is the verb form needed to complete the description of the committee's
decision. In (C), the prepositional phrase "of the resolution" appropriately speci es the
subject of the postponed discussion.

   The sentence may be corrected as follows:
After hours of futile debate, the committee has decided to postpone further
discussion of the resolution until its next meeting.

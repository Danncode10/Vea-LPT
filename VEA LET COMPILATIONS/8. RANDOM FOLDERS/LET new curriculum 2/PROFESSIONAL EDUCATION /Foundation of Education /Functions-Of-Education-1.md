FUNCTIONS OF ELEMENTARY, SECONDARY
AND HIGHER EDUCATION

Functions of Education

	Education in this country is supported by the public for a definite and noble purpose. The functions of education vary as the aims of education vary. Once the goals of education are known, the functions of education can be determined or formulated. The functions of education are therefore influenced by the aims of education set up by society and in part by the nature of the pupils to be educated.

	In the field of education today, there are two conflicting educational theories and practices: formalism and progressivism. The formalist emphasizes subject-matter which forms the basis of human culture. To the formalist group, it is prime duty of the teacher to pass these essentials to the succeeding generation in some systematic and logical order. This group tends to stress mastery of subject-matter, discipline, and logical organization and presentation.

	Progressive education places its emphasis upon the learner, his interests and desires, upon freedom rather than upon the subject-matter.

	The two points of view differ as to the true functions of education. To the formalist the fundamental function of education is the mastery of subject-matter which is based on the culture and traditions of the people. To the progressive group, the function is to provide a way for present living. To this group, education is essentially growth in the direction of freedom, democracy, tolerance and cooperative living and a mutual recognition of the welfare of one another.

Functions of the Philippine Elementary School

	The Philippine elementary school consists of four years of primary grades and two years of intermediate grades. The elementary grades are designed to give fundamental education to all children irrespective of race, sex and social status. It is in this unit of his schooling that the child is introduced to the culture and moral heritage of the race which is worthy of transmission and perpetuation. It is in this stage that he gains knowledge, skills, habits, abilities and attitudes essential to good citizenship in a democracy. 

	The functions of elementary education in relation to our general educational aims are the following:

Love of country – it is the function of the elementary school to develop in the individual love of country, which calls for love and loyalty to the democratic ideals, civic responsibility and civic righteousness, willingness to serve the community and the nation and sympathetic understanding of other nations and peoples and their contribution to world civilization.

The Code of Civic and Ethical Principles requires our schools to inculcate in the youth of our country the following precepts:

“Love your country for it is the home of our people, the seat of your affections, and the source of our happiness and well-being. Its defense is your primary duty. Be ready at all times to sacrifice and die for it if necessary.

Respect the Constitution which is the expression of your sovereign will. The government is your government. It has been established for your safety and welfare. Obey the laws and see that they are observed by all and that public officials comply with their duties.

Pay your taxes willingly and promptly. Citizenship implies not only rights but also obligations.

Safeguard the purity of suffrage and abide by the decisions of the majority.

Live up to the noble traditions of our people. Venerate the memory of our heroes. Their lives point the way to duty and honor.”

2. Teach the duties of citizenship – It is the function of elementary school to teach the individual the duties of citizenship and to impress upon him the conviction that he is a citizen of a republic with duties and responsibilities. Helpfulness, observance of rules and regulations, and love for service are developed in citizenship training. The preparation of the individual for the intelligent exercise of suffrage and the performance of his duties is included in citizenship training. 
To be a good citizen, an individual must be of good character imbued with an abiding faith in Divine Providence; he must be one who can exercise self-control, who respects the right of others, who loves his country and appreciates her cultural and moral heritage, and who is imbued with love for other people; one who is trained for and engaged in a certain vocation with an honest purpose to serve himself, his family, his community and his country.

	Elementary education should aim to equip every child in school with the fundamental knowledge, habits, skills, abilities, attitudes and ideals that are generally regarded as essential for the unification and integration of members of democratic society and for effective and productive participation in the activities of that society.

3. Development of Moral Character – moral character as a general aim of education is a recognition of the importance of character education. The function at the elementary school to develop moral character by inculcating in the individual the ideals of proper conduct and effective personality, the concept of moral laws and their values. Moral character can be achieved through the development of proper habits, good understanding, skills for the common welfare and attitudes such as courtesy, respect and helpfulness to others.

The objectives in character development must be correlated with the academic and vocational subject in the curriculum. Character education should utilize all possible activities in life which involve honesty, helpfulness, politeness, fairness, loyalty, cooperation and other social traits.

The most potent factor in the teaching of character is undoubtedly the teacher’s example. It is, therefore, essential that the teacher makes himself a model worthy of emulation.

4. Development of Personal Discipline – the elementary school to foster personal discipline by developing in the individual qualities by which he will be disposed to respect the rights of others; to make the individual appreciate the fundamental rights of man which should be preserved at any cost; to inculcate also the ideal of democracy.

Personal discipline in a democracy demands self-respect, self-direction, and self-control. Personal discipline in the elementary school can be best developed through proper direction and guidance. 
The teacher, should as much as possible, create learning situations or use socialized methods of teaching wherein self-control, cooperation, respect for the rights of others, and fair play are called for. Group projects, group games, and other social activities are good means of fostering personal discipline in elementary school children. 

Development of scientific, technological and vocational efficiency – it is the function of the elementary school to give the individual training in a certain vocation to enable him to earn an honest livelihood and to contribute to the economic well-being of the country.

To develop vocational efficiency, our elementary schools offer courses in carpentry and woodworking, basket-making, black smiting, and gardening for boys. Girls are given training in sewing, cooking, housekeeping and other domestic arts. 

Functions of Secondary Schools

	It is the function of secondary schools in the Philippines, “to continue on a higher plane the integrating function of education and at the same time to provide opportunity for the development of special abilities through the exploration and discovery of students’ interest, abilities and attitudes.

	The functions of secondary school differ from the functions of the elementary school in degree of emphasis received and in their adjustments to the age levels of pupils in each division. Each must meet the needs of its respective age group. Both the elementary and secondary schools are concerned principally with the general education of the citizens; their programs are centered around the general aims and both recognize that education is a  continuous process.

	The other functions of elementary school are hereby presented:

Guidance function – the central function of any school is guidance. Guidance is an organized service designed to give systematic aid to the pupils in making adjustments to various types of problems, which they must meet. Guidance in the high school covers the educational, vocational, moral, social and personal aspects of guidance.
The first part of guidance function of the secondary school is to explore the student’s interest, aptitude, and ability and what the student may need to know in order to prepare himself for a vocation 
The secondary school should concentrate on some vocational possibilities for those pupils who will not remain long in school. A secondary school guidance program places the teacher in a central position as counselor. 

2. Instruction function – continuing the general education gained in the elementary school to the point of complete mastery and wider application as the individual matures is another important function of secondary school. The high school must serve as the agency for continuing the general education for a higher level until necessary habits, skills and knowledge are firmly established.

According to Dr, Osias, “the purpose of high school must necessarily be that of assisting elementary school graduates to supplement and complement the functional and pre-vocational training which they have hitherto acquired, so that they may effect a better and more conscious adjustment to their social environment”.

Preparatory function – that the secondary school has preparatory function cannot be denied. This function is merely one phase of the adjustive function, having reference to those pupils preparing to continue their education in some higher institution of learning. Our secondary school does not only train the individual to meet life situations but also provides training and background in preparation for more independent study in a higher institution of learning.

Recreative function – the recreative function of secondary school refers to the development of refined tastes or attitudes, habits of conduct, interests and power of appreciation. It is concerned with the experience and expression of feeling and emotion. The recreative function is not fulfilled unless learning activities lead to the development of proper attitudes, interests, habits of conduct and appreciation.

Conserving Function – the secondary school serves as an agency for the conservation of our social heritage, social concepts and values which our people regards as their ideals and as the bases of our stability as a nation. It serves also as a stabilizing institution to conserve the national traits which make for permanence of the social order. The Christian principles and practices, our love for peace, freedom, and independence are traits which we can be justly proud.

Functions of Higher Education or University

	The college or university must serve the needs of society for more and more specialized and technically trained professionals in various fields if endeavor. College education also provides these professionals and technicians with their means of living. 

	To provide general education is another function of a college or university. The aim of general or liberal education is to develop the total personality of the individual and to prepare him for effective participation in a democratic society. General education includes the development of power to think clearly, the ability to communicate ideas effectively, and to stimulate wise judgment and the capacity to discriminate moral and spiritual values.

	It is also the function of colleges and universities to serve as instrument for moral regeneration and for social progress. They must engage more in the search for truth, instead of confining their functions to the transmission of knowledge alone. 

	Another function of our colleges and universities is to train leaders in the field of economic development.

	The specific functions of a college or university, to be worthy of its mission are the following:

1. Research – it is a solemn function of a university to undertake research. Section 5 of the Republic Act No. 1124 states that the government shall give encouragement to colleges and universities for the promotion of research especially in the field of industry, Philippine arts and culture, science and social problems.

	Research advances the frontier of knowledge and the appreciation of knowledge. Results of research can be used to speed up economic progress and expand social services. The college or university must establish a research center which should develop a comprehensive program of research and work in close coordination with industries.

	2. Instructional Function – most of our colleges and universities are teaching colleges and universities. Continuing general education gained in high school to the point of complete mastery and wider application as the individual matures is another function of a college or university. It is in college or university where the students formulate their life purposes and life objectives.

	3. Specialization Function – the instructional function of a university is designed to develop skills and techniques of the trade or profession. It is the function of the university today to provide students with professional means of living.

	4. Conserving Function –the college or university serves as an agency for the conservation of our heritage, social concepts and values which our people regards as their ideals and as the bases of our stability as a nation. It serves to conserve the national social traits which make for permanence of the social order.

	5. Recreative Function – refers to the development of refined tastes or attitude, habits of conduct, interests, and power of appreciation. Philippine history and government, literature and arts are included in the college curriculum to instill feeling of appreciation, admiration, and pride of our valuable heritage. The more we understand our culture – traditions, mores, and processes, the better  we will appreciate and value them.

	6. Guidance and counseling function – high school and college students need both direct and indirect guidance and counseling. The guidance program must include provisions for (1) individual and group counseling, (2) cooperation within the college and other agencies, (3) vocational placement and follow-up, and (4) research in field related to the student welfare and educational opportunities.

	7. Preparatory function – This is closely related to instructional function. As long as students pursue general education beyond secondary level, the preparatory function of the College of Liberal Arts cannot be neglected.

	8. Extension service –The function of the university is to develop knowledge needed, and to apply useful knowledge in the solution of society’s major ills through the establishment of extension service program. To accomplish the above specific function, the college or university must come into closer contact with the community and the outside world it serves. Faculty and students should no longer remain an ivory tower.

Articulation of Elementary and Secondary School Program

	The history of education in this country reveals to us that the elementary school and the high school have the same educational objectives. They must have similar aims and functions in form and in spirit. Through these two institutions, the privileges of lower and higher education are extended to the masses. The aims and functions of our elementary and secondary schools are not mutually independent, but are related and interdependent.

	Articulation, however, denotes a vertical tie-up of a course to an earlier or later course. Articulation implies the close coupling of courses and educational experiences in sequential manner to obtain continuity of student development.

Articulation of the School Program Needs Guidance

	Articulation of elementary and secondary school curricula calls for proper guidance in both units of our school system. The entire educative process is a guidance activity.  Good teaching means stimulating, directing, guiding, and evaluating learning. Functional integration of guidance and teaching is necessary in any school.

	School guidance places the teacher in the central position of counselor, a. leader whose chief aim is to help the pupil understand himself and his needs, to guide the pupil through worthy experiences, and to help him develop the ability to live creatively and effectively in society.

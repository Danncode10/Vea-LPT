• Edith Lopez-Tiempo, poet, fiction writer, teacher and literary critic was a Filipino
  writer in the English language. She was conferred the National Artist Award for
  Literature in 1999. Her poems are intricate verbal transfigurations of significant
  experiences as revealed, in two of her much-anthologized
  pieces, "Halaman" and "Bonsai.” Together with her late husband, writer and critic
  Edilberto K. Tiempo, they founded (in 1962) and directed the Silliman National
  Writers Workshop in Dumaguete City.
• Paz Márquez-Benítez was a Filipina short-story writer, educator and editor. During
  her career as a writer, Marquez-Benitez wrote short stories critical of American
  Imperialism. She is most known by her short story Dead Stars (1925).
• Gilda Cordero-Fernando was a Filipino writer, visual artist, fashion designer and
  publisher. Some of her works were published in two collections of short
  stories: The Butcher, The Baker and The Candlestick Maker (1962) and A
  Wilderness of Sweets (1973). These books were later compiled and reissued as
  the Story Collection (1994). During this period, she also gained acclaim for her
  column "Tempest in a Teapot" in the Manila Chronicle, before the newspaper was
  shut down during Martial Law.

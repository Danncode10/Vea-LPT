Filipino Traits
Meaning
Kanya-kanya o Tayu-tayo
Protecting personal or small group interests over the common good; a self-centered or clannish mentality.
Ningas Cogon
Starting projects with intense enthusiasm but quickly losing interest and leaving them unfinished, like a wildfire that burns out fast.
Mañana Habit
Procrastinating or putting off tasks for tomorrow ("mañana") what can be done today.
Ethnocentric
Believing one's own local culture, regional group, or tribe is superior to all others.
Xeno centric
Preferring foreign lifestyles, products, and ideas over local ones, often driven by colonial mentality.
Extreme personalism
Viewing the world through personal relationships, making it hard to separate professional duties from family or friendship ties.
Bahala na
A fatalistic "come what may" attitude, leaving outcomes to fate, God, or chance when facing difficult situations. (ambivalent)
Porma over substance
Prioritizing outward appearance, status symbols, and flashiness over actual quality, depth, or functionality, (novice learner) while substance over porma (expert learner)
Remedyo Attitude
Improvising creative, quick-fix solutions using whatever is available to solve a problem temporarily.
Cheerful
Maintaining a positive, smiling, and optimistic outlook on life even during severe crises or disasters.

Group Name

Dawn Man

Felipe Landa Jocano’s Core Population Theory (Evolution Theory)
Component
Description
Distinct Features
Main Premise
No Migration Waves
• Rejects the idea of distinct, successive waves of migrant races. • Proposes a single, regional core population with a shared base culture.
Process
Evolutionary Differentiation
• Humans evolved gradually over hundreds of thousands of years. • Environmental demands and geographic isolation created distinct ethnic groups.
Stage 1
Formative Phase
• Use of crude stone tools. • Reliance on hunting, foraging, and basic survival techniques.
Stage 2
Incipient Phase
• Introduction of early metal tools. • Development of pottery and early manufacturing. • Emergence of local customs.
Stage 3
Emergent Phase
• Growth of regional trade networks and maritime skills. • Solidification of a distinct native Filipino cultural identity.
Evidence
Archaeological Finds
• Tabon Man (Palawan) proves an ancient human presence. • Shared pottery and burial patterns show a unified cultural foundation.

PRE-SPANISH PERIOD SOCIAL CLASS (MAGMATINS)




Social Class
Who They Were
Simple Explanation
Maginoo
The Royals / Leaders
• The highest class of nobles, chiefs, and rulers (like the Datu). • They made the laws, protected the village, and held all the power.
Maharlika
The Elite Warriors
• Loyal fighters who protected the leaders during times of war. • They did not pay taxes and were highly respected for their bravery.
Timawa
The Ordinary Free People
• Regular citizens like farmers, fishermen, merchants, and craftsmen. • They were completely free, owned land, and kept the money they made.
Aliping Namamahay
The House-Owning Servants
• Workers who lived in their own homes with their own families. • They only came over to help the master farm or build houses when asked.
Aliping Saguiguilid
The Live-In Servants
• Workers who lived inside the master's house and owned no property. • They served full-time, but they could still buy their freedom if they saved enough money.

Trade & Alliances
Barter & Sanduguan
• Barter: Trading goods for other goods (no money used). • Sanduguan: A blood compact ritual to seal a friendship or alliance.
Belief System

Marriage Customs

• Betrothal: An arranged engagement to be married. • Dowry: The gift (money or property) given by the groom to the bride's family.

SOCIAL CLASS DURNG THE COLONIZATION (PIMPI)


Social Class
Who They Were
Simple Explanation
Peninsulares
Spaniards born in Spain
• The highest and most powerful social class. • Held the top positions in the government, military, and Catholic Church.
Insulares
Spaniards born in the Philippines
• Pure Spanish blood, but looked down upon by Peninsulares for being born in the colonies. • Often referred to as "Filipinos" during that specific time period.
Mestizo
Mixed-race people
• People of mixed ancestry (usually Spanish-Filipino or Chinese-Filipino). • Often became wealthy merchants, landowners, and educated individuals.
Principalia
The native ruling elite
• The noble class of native Filipinos, often made up of former Datus and local officials. • Given special privileges like exempting themselves from forced labor.
Indio
The native Filipinos
• The lowest social class, consisting of pure-blooded indigenous Malays. • Faced heavy taxation, discrimination, and forced labor (polo y servicio).

RISE OF NATIONALISM

Date / Era 
Event
Key Figures Involved
Historical Significance
1860s
Secularisasyon (Secularization Movement)
Fr. Pedro Pelaez, Fr. José Burgos
The campaign to allow local Filipino secular priests to run parishes instead of Spanish friars, sowing the earliest seeds of structural nationalist identity.
Jan 20, 1872
Cavite Mutiny
Sgt. Fernando La Madrid, Filipino soldiers & workers
A brief uprising of around 200 soldiers and yard workers at the Fort San Felipe arsenal triggered by the sudden removal of their labor tax exemptions.
Feb 17, 1872
Execution of GOMBURZA
Fr. Mariano Gomez, Fr. José Burgos, Fr. Jacinto Zamora
The Spanish colonial authorities falsely implicated and executed the three priests via garrote, serving as the emotional catalyst for Philippine nationalism.
1880s – 1895
Propaganda Movement
Jose Rizal, Marcelo H. del Pilar, Graciano López Jaena
A peaceful cultural, political, and literary campaign launched by Filipino expatriates (Ilustrados) in Spain to demand assimilation and equal rights.
July 3, 1892
Founding of La Liga Filipina
Dr. Jose Rizal
A short-lived civic society established by Rizal in Manila to unite the archipelago and peacefully lobby for reforms.
July 7, 1892
Founding of the Katipunan (KKK)
Andres Bonifacio, Valentin Diaz, Ladislao Diwa, Deodato Arellano
A secret revolutionary society formed immediately after Rizal's arrest, seeking total separation and violent independence from Spain.
Dec 30, 1896
Death of Rizal
Dr. Jose Rizal, Camilo Polavieja
Rizal was executed by a Spanish firing squad at Bagumbayan for allegedly instigating the armed revolution, fueling widespread rebellion.
March 22, 1897
Tejeros Convention (TC)
Emilio Aguinaldo, Andres Bonifacio
A chaotic meeting between the Magdalo and Magdiwang factions where Aguinaldo was elected president, replacing Bonifacio’s leadership over the revolution.
May 10, 1897
Death of Bonifacio
Andres Bonifacio, Procopio Bonifacio
Following the friction at the Tejeros Convention, Bonifacio and his brother were arrested, tried for treason, and executed in Cavite.
Dec 14–15, 1897
Pact of Biak-na-Bato ("Sold KKK")
Emilio Aguinaldo, Gov. Gen. Primo de Rivera
A truce that paused the revolution; Aguinaldo agreed to dissolve his bases and go into exile in Hong Kong in exchange for amnesty and a financial payout.
Dec 10, 1898
Treaty of Paris
American and Spanish Diplomats
The international accord that officially ended Spanish rule; Spain sold the Philippines to the United States for $20 million.

Complete Katipunan (KKK) Historical Fact Sheet
Category
Component / Title
Complete Historical Details & Context
Identity & Origins
Official Name
Kataas-taasang, Kagalang-galangang Katipunan ng mga Anak ng Bayan (Supreme and Venerable Society of the Children of the Nation).

Founding Info
Founded on July 7, 1892, at 72 Azcarraga Street, Tondo, Manila by Andres Bonifacio, Valentin Diaz, Teodoro Plata, Ladislao Diwa, and Deodato Arellano.
Leadership Timeline
First President
Deodato Arellano (1892–1893) — Served as the first Supremo.

Second President
Roman Basa (1893–1895) — Served as the second Supremo.

Third President
Andres Bonifacio (1895–1897) — The final Supremo and primary driver of the revolution.
Core Objectives (PMC)
Political (P)
Achieve total separation and independence of the Philippines from Spain through armed revolution.

Moral (M)
Teach good manners, hygiene, high morals, and actively fight against religious fanaticism.

Civic (C)
Promote self-help and defend the poor, the weak, and the oppressed.
Literature & Press
Guidebook
Kartilya ng Katipunan — The official primer containing 14 lessons, written by Emilio Jacinto (honored as the Utak ng Katipunan).

Newspaper
Kalayaan (Liberty) — The underground newspaper used to spread revolutionary ideas and rapidly increase recruitment.
Prominent Figures
Gatekeeper (Lakambini)
Gregoria de Jesus — Wife of Bonifacio; served as the keeper of the society's secret documents, ledger, and official seals.

Mother of the KKK
Melchora Aquino (Tandang Sora) — Provided food, shelter, and medical care to wounded revolutionists. (Note: Common mix-up in notes is Teodora Aquino).

The Informant (Taksil)
Teodoro Patiño — Exposed the secret existence of the KKK to Fr. Mariano Gil after a dispute with a fellow worker at the Diario de Manila press.
The Outbreak
The Cry (Sigaw)
The Cry of Balintawak / Pugad Lawin took place at the house of Melchora Aquino. Katipuneros tore their cedulas (tax certificates) to declare open revolt against Spain.

Greatest Love: Rizal’s cousin and his fiancée for over a decade. She served as his main inspiration for the tragic character of Maria Clara in Noli Me Tangere. Their relationship ended when her mother forced her to marry an English engineer, Charles Kipping.

O-Sei-San (Seiko Usui)

1888
Gertrude Beckett
The London Stay: The eldest daughter of Rizal's landlord. She fell deeply in love with him while helping him with his clay sculptures. Rizal left London abruptly for Paris to avoid leading her on, as his feelings were not mutual.

American-Era Philippine Acts Master Summary
Act Name
Year
Core Purpose & Historical Impact
a. Hare-Hawes-Cutting Act
1933
• First US law promising Philippine independence. • Set a 10-year transition period. • Rejected by Manuel L. Quezon. • Opposed due to US military base clauses.
b. Tydings-McDuffie Act
1934
• Formally replaced the Hare-Hawes-Cutting Act. • Approved by the Philippine Legislature. • Established the Philippine Commonwealth. • Successfully led to independence in 1946.
c. Gabaldon Act
1907
• Written by Assemblyman Isauro Gabaldon. • Funded public school construction nationwide. • Created iconic architectural heritage structures. • Built foundations for modern public education.
d. Payne-Aldrich Act
1909
• Established partial free trade relations. • Allowed duty-free American goods into the Philippines. • Placed strict quotas on major Philippine exports (sugar/tobacco).
e. Underwood-Simmons Act
1913
• Passed directly after the Payne-Aldrich Act. • Established full free trade relations. • Completely abolished quota limitations on Philippine exports to the US.
f. Jones Law
1916
• First formal and official US commitment to grant independence. • Stated independence would come as soon as a stable government was built. • Replaced the Philippine Organic Act of 1902. • Created the first fully elected all-Filipino bicameral legislature.
g. Bell Trade Act
1946
• Regulated post-WWII economic relations after independence. • Granted "parity rights" allowing Americans equal access to resources. • Tied a $800 million US rehabilitation fund to its ratification. • Fixed the exchange rate at 2 Philippine Pesos to 1 US Dollar.

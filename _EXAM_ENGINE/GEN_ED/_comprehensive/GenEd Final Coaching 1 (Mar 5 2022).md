Basilio Augustín y Dávila was briefly a Spanish Governor-General of the Philippines, from April 11 to July 24,
1898. During his tenure, the Spanish–American War began, Spanish forces were decisively defeated by the
American Navy in the Battle of Manila Bay. This defeat led to the return of revolutionary leader Emilio
Aguinaldo from exile and the beginning of the second phase of the Philippine Revolution, during which, on June
12, 1898, Aguinaldo issued the Philippine Declaration of Independence.
Rafael Gerónimo Cayetano Izquierdo y Gutiérrez served as Governor-General of the Philippines from 4 April
1871 to 8 January 1873. He was famous for his use of "Iron Fist" type of government, contradicting the liberal
government of his predecessor, Carlos María de la Torre y Navacerrada. He was the Governor-General during the
1872 Cavite mutiny which led to execution of 41 of the mutineers, including the Gomburza martyrs.

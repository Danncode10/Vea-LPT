Linear Models of Communication

Aristotle’s Model (300 B.C.)
Elements
Highlights 
One-way process focused on public speaking.
Elements:
 1. Speaker – source of the message
 2. Message – what is being said
 3. Audience – target listener
 4. Effect – influence on the audience
➤ Considered the first communication model in Western tradition.
➤ Designed for rhetoric and persuasion.
➤ Example: A politician giving a speech to gain public support.

Lasswell’s Model (1948)
Elements
Highlights 
Process of communication through five guiding questions:
 1. Who – the sender or communicator
 2. Says what – the message
 3. In which channel – the medium or method used
 4. To whom – the audience or receiver
 5. With what effect – the result or impact of the message
➤ Used in mass communication and propaganda during WWII.
➤ Focused on media effects.
➤ Example: A TV ad influencing consumer behavior.

Shannon-Weaver Model (1949)
Elements
Highlights 
Known as the Mathematical Model of Communication. 
Elements:
 1. Information Source – origin of the message
 2.Transmitter – encodes the message into signals
 3. Channel – medium through which the signal travels
 4. Noise Source – any distortion or barrier
 5. Receiver – decodes the signal
 6. Destination – intended target of the message
 7. Feedback – later added to reflect two-way communication
➤ Created by engineers to improve telephone systems.
➤ Introduced concept of “noise”.
➤ Example: A phone call with static disrupting message clarity.

Berlo’s SMCR Model (1960)
Elements
Highlights 
Highlights importance of communication skills, attitudes, knowledge, and social systems.

SMCR stands for:
 1. Source – includes communication skills, attitudes, knowledge, social system, and culture
 2. Message – includes content, elements, treatment, structure, and code
 3. Channel – senses used in communication: hearing, seeing, touching, smelling, and tasting
 4. Receiver – has the same components as the source

➤ Emphasizes that communication effectiveness depends on matching source and receiver backgrounds.
➤ Example: Teacher (source) communicating effectively with students.







Interactional Models of Communication

Interactional Models of Communication
Elements
Highlights 
Communication is a two-way process where sender and receiver alternate roles. Introduced "Field of Experience" for shared understanding.
➤ First to propose communication as a reciprocal process.
➤ Field of experience = shared background.
➤ Example: Teacher-student Q&A session.

Osgood-Schramm Model (1954)
Elements
Highlights 
Developed by William Scramm, known as the father of mass communication
A circular model of communication. Both sender and receiver are simultaneous encoders, decoders, and interpreters.
➤ Emphasizes feedback and continuous exchange.
➤ Used in interpersonal and small-group communication.
➤ Example: Face-to-face dialogue.

Transactional Models of Communication

White’s Model (1960)
Elements
Highlights 
Involves 8 stages of communication: Thinking, Symbolizing, Expressing, Transmitting, Receiving, Decoding, Feedbacking, Monitoring. Shows communication as a sequential process.
➤ First to use the term “feedbacking”.
➤ Recognizes self-monitoring in communication.
➤ Example: Radio DJ evaluating audience response.

Dance’s Helix Model (1967)
Elements
Highlights 
Communication is continuous, dynamic, and ever-expanding, like a helix. Combines linearity and circularity.
➤ Emphasizes time and development in communication.
➤ Our communication today is influenced by our past.
➤ Example: A friendship growing through continuous interaction.

Barnlund’s Model (1970)
Elements
Highlights 
Multi-layered, real-time communication. Both participants are senders and receivers simultaneously. 
Elements:
 1. P (Person) – each communicator
 2. D (Decoding) – interpreting incoming cues
 3. E (Encoding) – creating outgoing messages
 4. Cpr (Public Cues) – environmental or external signs like gestures, tone
 5. Cpv (Private Cues) – internal or personal thoughts, feelings, and intentions
 6. Cbeh.nv – Nonverbal behavioral cues (e.g., facial expressions, gestures)
 7. Vbeh.v – Verbal behavioral cues (spoken words, tone)
 8. M – Message being communicated
➤ Best reflects real-life communication.
➤ Includes nonverbal cues, context, and simultaneous feedback.
➤ Example: Group discussion or team meeting.

Nature of Communication
	•	It is a continuous process
	•	It involves two or more people
	•	It is expressed through verbal and non-verbal means
Functions of Communication
	•	Control – regulates behavior and maintains order
	•	Social Interaction – facilitates relationships and social connections
	•	Motivation – inspires and encourages action or behavior
	•	Emotional Expression – conveys feelings and emotions
	•	Information Dissemination – shares knowledge and data
Globalization And Communication
	•	Globalization – the process of spreading products, ideas, technology, and jobs across countries and cultures
	•	Global Village – a term by Herbert Marshall McLuhan (1964) referring to the world becoming interconnected through telecommunications
	•	Culture – shared patterns of behavior, beliefs, values, and norms of a group or society
Characteristics Of Culture
	•	Culture is Learned – not inherited; acquired through interaction and understanding
	•	Culture is Shared – involves group behavior and social interactions
	•	Culture is Multi-faceted – includes cultural universals and norms influencing all aspects of life
	•	Culture is Dynamic – constantly changing due to time, experience, and external influence
	•	Cultural Identities are Overlapping – multiple cultural influences may coexist, blend, or sometimes clash
Adaptation To New Cultures
	•	Cultural Diversity – the presence of multiple distinct cultures within a group or society
	•	Cultural Integration – cultures exchange traits and influence one another while retaining their uniqueness
	•	Cultural Assimilation – adopting the dominant culture completely, sometimes losing original identity
	•	Multiculturalism – coexistence of multiple distinct cultures within a single society
	•	Cultural Accommodation – adapting to a new culture while preserving one’s original culture
	•	Separation – resisting interaction or integration with another culture; cultural isolation

Kinds Of Responses To Cultural Differences
	•	Avoiding – refusal to engage or comply with cultural practices that conflict with one’s own principles
	•	Accommodating – accepting the differences in others’ ethical systems
	•	Forcing – imposing one’s own ethical views, insisting they are superior
	•	Educating–Persuading – explaining and promoting one’s ethical principles in an effort to influence others
	•	Negotiating–Compromising – mutual agreement where both parties give up something to resolve a conflict
	•	Collaboration–Problem Solving – working together to understand and solve issues across cultures
Impact Of Technology On Communication
	•	Provides access to vast information and knowledge
	•	Facilitates forming new connections and friendships
	•	Keeps people connected regardless of distance
	•	Introduces cultural diversity into daily life
	•	Enhances collaboration between individuals of different cultures
	•	Can lead to social isolation and reduced face-to-face interaction
	•	May encourage the creation of “second lives” or online identities
Culture Shock 
Culture Shock – a feeling of disorientation and discomfort due to sudden immersion in an unfamiliar culture

Four Stages of Culture Shock
	•	Honeymoon Phase – excitement and fascination with the new culture
	•	Negotiation Phase – feelings of frustration and homesickness begin; stereotypes may serve as a coping mechanism  3 Main Types of Reaction   a. Rejecters – isolate themselves and avoid interaction with the new culture  b. Adopters – fully accept and embrace the new culture  c. Cosmopolitans – adapt to the new culture while maintaining their original cultural identity
	•	Adjustment Phase – typically occurs 6 to 12 months after exposure; individuals begin to adapt and feel more comfortable
	•	Reverse Culture Shock – difficulty readjusting to one’s original culture after adapting to a new one
Local And Global Communication In Multicultural Settings
	•	Multiculturalism – a society made up of diverse cultural and ethnic groups
	•	 Intercultural Communication – effective interaction between people from different cultural backgrounds
Forms Of Intercultural Communication
	•	Interracial – communication between people of different races
	•	Interethnic – communication among people of different ethnic backgrounds
	•	International – communication between representatives from different nations
	•	Intracultural – communication among people of the same racial or ethnic group
Context In Culture
	•	High-Context Culture – communication relies heavily on nonverbal cues, traditions, and implicit understanding
	•	Low-Context Culture – communication is direct, clear, and explicit
Cultural Awareness And Sensitivity
	•	Cultural Awareness – understanding that cultural differences exist
	•	Cultural Sensitivity – recognizing and respecting cultural differences without assuming superiority
Various Cultural And Intercultural Modes
	•	Eye Contact – varies in meaning depending on the culture (e.g., respect, aggression, honesty)
	•	Hand Gestures – gestures can have different meanings across cultures
	•	Greetings – may include handshakes, bows, or verbal expressions
Communication Issues
	•	Sexism – discrimination based on gender
	•	Gender Sensitivity – awareness and avoidance of gender-based discrimination in communication
	•	Racism – belief in the superiority of one race over another; divides people based on physical characteristics
Political Correctness
Adopting language and behavior aimed at avoiding offense or disadvantage to individuals or groups in society

Language Variety 
Language Variety – different forms of a language used by various groups
	•	Dialect – regional or social variation of a language
	•	Regional Dialect – based on location
	•	Sociolect – based on social class or group
	•	Ethnolect – used by a specific ethnic group
	•	Idiolect – individual’s unique speech style

	•	Accent – way of pronouncing words
	•	Mother Tongue – first language of a community
	•	Pidgin – simplified mix of languages used to communicate without a shared language
Language Registers 
Language Registers – levels or styles of language use
	•	Frozen – fixed, ritualistic (e.g., prayers, oaths)
	•	Formal – structured, professional (e.g., speeches, reports)
	•	Consultative – interaction with expert or authority (e.g., teacher-student)
	•	Casual – everyday conversation among friends
	•	Intimate – private, personal talk (e.g., between close friends or family)

MALAYUNING KOMUNIKASYON SA FILIPINO

Intelektwalisasyon
Barayti ng Filipino na itinuturo sa mga Filipino sa lahat ng antas ng edukasyon mula elementarya hanggang kolehiyo (tulad ng Filipino sa paaralan).

Mga Dimensyon Ng Pagpaplanong Pangwika
	•	Seleksyon – pagpili ng wikang gagamitin (halimbawa, pagpili ng Filipino bilang pambansang wika)
	•	Estardisasyon – pagtatakda ng tuntunin sa wastong gamit (pagbuo ng tuntunin sa balarila at bokabularyo)
	•	Propagasyon – pagpapalaganap ng wikang napili (pagsasama ng Filipino sa kurikulum)
	•	Kultibasyon – pag-aangkop ng wika bilang bahagi ng kultura at lipunan (paggamit ng Filipino sa araw-araw na buhay at kultura)
Teorya Ng Pinagmulan Ng Wika
	•	Bow-wow – wika ay hango sa tunog ng hayop (tunog ng aso, pusa)
	•	Ding-dong – wika ay hango sa tunog ng bagay (tunog ng kampana)
	•	Poo-pooh – wika ay hango sa emosyon (sighs, daing)
	•	Yo-he-ho – wika ay hango sa puwersa o pagsusumikap (tunog ng sabayan na paggawa)
	•	Mama – tunog mula sa unang pantig ng "mother" (pagbigkas ng ina ng bata)
	•	Sing-song – wika ay musikal o may melodiya (pag-awit ng mga sinaunang tao)
	•	Hey You! – wika ay ginagamit para sa pagkakakilanlan at pagtawag (pagtawag sa kaibigan)
	•	Coo coo – tunog na gaya ng sa sanggol (tunog ng baby cooing)
	•	Yum yum – tunog na kaugnay sa pagkain (tunog ng gutom)
	•	Babble Lucky – nagkataong mabuo ang mga tunog na nagkaroon ng kahulugan (pagbuo ng mga unang salita)
	•	Hocus Pocus – wika ay mahiwaga o mahikal (ritwal, panalangin)
	•	Eurekall – wika bilang tanda o alaala (pagkilala sa isang bagay)
	•	Tata – wika bilang paalam (pagsabi ng paalam)
Rehistro Ng Wika
	•	Lingua Franca – wikang ginagamit bilang pantulay
	•	Unibersal – English (halimbawa: komunikasyon sa buong mundo)
	•	Pambansa – Filipino (wikang pambansa ng Pilipinas)
	•	Rehiyonal – Ilocano, Cebuano (wikang ginagamit sa mga rehiyon)
	•	Dayalek – barayti ng wika batay sa lugar (halimbawa: Batangas Tagalog vs. Metro Manila Tagalog)
	•	Idyolek – natatanging paraan ng pagsasalita ng isang tao (style ng pagsasalita ng isang indibidwal)
	•	Sosyolek – wika batay sa grupo ng lipunan (wikang ginagamit ng mga kabataan, propesyonal)
	•	Ekolek – wikang ginagamit sa loob ng tahanan (wikang ginagamit ng pamilya)
	•	Etnolek – wika ng partikular na pangkat etniko (halimbawa: Kapampangan, Ifugao)
	•	Jargon – teknikal na salita sa isang propesyon o larangan (medical terms, legal terms)
	•	Pidgin – pinaghalong wika para sa komunikasyon ng magkaibang grupo (halimbawa: Bisaya-English pidgin)
	•	Creole – nativized o unang wika mula sa pidgin (halimbawa: Chavacano)
Antas Ng Wika
	•	Pormal – ginagamit sa opisyal na okasyon o sulatin
	•	Pambansa – salitang pang-araw-araw at kilala ng lahat (halimbawa: "upuan")
	•	Pampanitikan – mas mataas na antas at karaniwang ginagamit sa panitikan (halimbawa: "silya")
	•	Impormal – pang-araw-araw na gamit
	•	Balbal – salitang kalye (halimbawa: "jologs")
	•	Kolokyal – karaniwang salita na may halo ng ibang wika (halimbawa: Taglish, Enggalog)
	•	Lalawiganin – salita mula sa mga lalawigan (halimbawa: "kadiwa")
	•	Bulgar – bastos o di-kanais-nais na pananalita (halimbawa: mura)

Komunikasyon (Talastasan)
Pagpapalitan o pagbabahagi ng kaisipan, damdamin, o impormasyon sa pagitan ng dalawa o higit pang tao

Elemento Ng Komunikasyon

	•	Mensahe – ideya o damdaming nais iparating
	•	Tsanel – daluyan ng mensahe (halimbawa: pasalita, pasulat)
	•	Tagatanggap (Receiver) – tumatanggap ng mensahe
	•	Fidbak (Feedback) – tugon ng tagatanggap
Modelo Ng Komunikasyon
	•	Aristotle’s Model
	•	Speaker → Speech → Audience → Effect  (naka-sentro sa tagapagsalita at epekto sa tagapakinig)
	•	Berlo’s SMCR Model  Source → Message → Channel → Receiver  (kasama ang salik tulad ng attitude, skills, at kultura sa pagpapadala at pagtanggap ng mensahe)
	•	Schramm’s Model  Encoder-Interpreter-Decoder  (binibigyang-diin ang pagkakatulad ng karanasan ng tagapagsalita at tagapakinig)
Uri Ng Komunikasyon
	•	Berbal – ginagamitan ng salita (pasalita o pasulat)
	•	Di-berbal – hindi ginagamitan ng salita
	•	Proxemics – distansya o espasyo (lapit o layo ng kausap)
	•	Oculesics – galaw ng mata o paningin (eye contact o pag-iwas ng tingin)
	•	Pictics – ekspresyon ng mukha (pagngiti, pagkunot-noo)
	•	Chronemics – paggamit ng oras (maagang pagdating o pagka-late)
	•	Haptics – paggamit ng haplos o paghipo (tapik sa balikat)
	•	Olfactorics – paggamit ng pang-amoy (amoy ng pabango, pagkain)
	•	Objectics – gamit na may kahulugan sa komunikasyon (bag, cellphone)
	•	Colorics – gamit ng kulay (itim – pormal, pula – tapang)
	•	Iconics – simbolo o imaheng may kahulugan (logo, signage)
	•	Kinesics – kilos ng katawan (kumpas ng kamay, pagtango)
Antas Ng Komunikasyon
	•	Intrapersonal – pakikipag-usap sa sarili (pagninilay)
	•	Interpersonal – pakikipag-usap sa ibang tao (usap ng magkaibigan)
	•	Pampubliko – komunikasyong para sa nakararami (talumpati)
	•	Pang-media – gamit ang mass media (TV, radyo, social media)
Makrong Kasanayan Sa Pagkomunikasyon
	•	Pagbasa – pagbibigay-kahulugan sa mga simbolo o titik
Proseso ng Pagbasa:
	•	Persepsyon – pagkilala sa simbolo (letra/salita)
	•	Komprehensyon – pag-unawa sa binasa
	•	Reaksyon – opinyon o tugon sa binasa
	•	Asimilasyon – pagsanib ng bagong kaalaman sa dating kaalaman
	•	Konotasyon – di-tuwirang kahulugan, madalas masining o malikhain (puso – damdamin)
	•	Denotasyon – tuwirang kahulugan mula sa diksyunaryo (puso – bahagi ng katawan)




Teorya Ng Pagbasa
	•	Bottom-up – nagsisimula sa teksto; unti-unting pag-unawa (mula salita patungo sa kahulugan)
	•	Top-down – mula sa karanasan ng mambabasa patungo sa pag-unawa ng teksto
	•	Interaktib – pinagsamang top-down at bottom-up
	•	Iskima – paggamit ng dating kaalaman upang maunawaan ang bagong impormasyon
Uri Ng Pagbasa
	•	Scanning – paghahanap ng tiyak na impormasyon (numero, pangalan)
	•	Skimming – mabilisang pagbasa para sa pangkalahatang ideya (buod ng kwento)
	•	Pasaklaw (Intensive) – masinsinang pag-unawa sa nilalaman (analisis ng artikulo)
	•	Panlibang – pagbasa para sa aliw o kasiyahan (komiks, nobela)
Pagsulat 
Pagsulat – pagsasatitik ng mga kaisipan, damdamin, at ideya sa malinaw, maayos, at makabuluhang paraan

Layunin Ng Pagsulat
	•	Persweysib – nanghihikayat (hal. patalastas, sanaysay na nangungumbinsi)
	•	Informativ – nagbibigay ng impormasyon (hal. ulat, pamanahong papel)
	•	Malikhain – pampanitikan o masining (hal. tula, maikling kwento)
Uri Ng Pagsulat
	•	Akademik – pormal, may estruktura at lohika, pang-intelektwal (hal. tesis, replektibong sanaysay)
	•	Journalistik – balita at mga artikulo para sa pahayagan (hal. editoryal, lathalain)
	•	Teknikal – tumutugon sa suliraning kognitibo o saykolohikal (hal. manwal, feasibility study)
	•	Malikhain – imahinatibo, masining (hal. dula, tula, kwento)
	•	Referensyal – batay sa sanggunian, ginagamit sa pananaliksik (hal. bibliograpiya, citation)
	•	Propesyonal – eksklusibo para sa isang larangan o propesyon (hal. legal na dokumento, medikal na ulat)
Mga Dapat Isaalang-alang Sa Pagsulat
	•	Kaisahan – isang pangunahing diwa
	•	Kasapatan – sapat na detalye at paliwanag
	•	Kaugnayan – lohikal at maayos na daloy
	•	Kalinawan – malinaw at madaling maunawaan
	•	Diin – pagbibigay-pansin sa mahalagang punto

Proseso Ng Pagsulat

	•	Drafting – unang pagsulat ng ideya
	•	Revising – pagrerebisa ng nilalaman, organisasyon, at estilo
	•	Polishing – pagwawasto ng grammar, spelling, at estruktura

Controversies in Philippine History 

First Mass in the Philippines 
	•	 Ano 'to?: Debate tungkol sa saan talaga ginanap ang unang misa sa Pilipinas.
	•	Bakit kontrobersyal?: May magkaibang account kung saan nangyari — sa Mazaua o Butuan.
	•	Ano'ng nangyari?
	•	Antonio Pigafetta (chronicler of Magellan): Sinabing naganap ang misa sa Mazaua (near Humonhon, Eastern Visayas).
	•	Fr. Urdaneta: Sinasabing nagtangkang tumigil ang mga Kastila sa Camiguin at Masava, pero napadpad sa Butuan, tapos sa Bohol dahil sa agos ng dagat.
	•	Bottom line: Wala pang final proof kung Mazaua o Butuan, pero Limasawa sa Southern Leyte ang opisyal na kinikilala sa kasalukuyan (ng NHCP).
Cavite Mutiny (1872)
	•	Ano 'to?: Rebellion ng ilang Pilipinong sundalo at manggagawa sa Fort San Felipe, Cavite, laban sa mga Kastila.
	•	Bakit kontrobersyal?: Magkaibang pananaw kung bakit nangyari ang mutiny at kung sino ang sangkot.
	•	Ano'ng nangyari?
	•	Jose Montero Vidal (Spanish writer): Sabi niya, planong asasinuhin ang mga opisyal, pero napagkamalan lang ang fireworks sa Sampaloc fiesta bilang signal ng pag-aalsa.
	•	Gen. Rafael Izquierdo (Spanish governor): Isinisi sa mga paring Pilipino at layunin daw nitong pabagsakin ang pamahalaan.
	•	Dr. Trinidad Pardo de Tavera (Filipino scholar): Maliit lang daw ang mutiny at ginamit lang ito ng Kastila at mga prayle para mapatahimik ang mga Pilipinong repormista, gaya nina GomBurZa, na pinatay pagkatapos nito.
	•	Bottom line: Ang mutiny ay naging dahilan ng pagbitay sa GomBurZa, na nag-udyok sa kilusang propaganda at sa diwang makabayan ng mga Pilipino.
Cry of Pugad Lawin (1896)
	•	Ano 'to?: Simbolo ng simula ng himagsikan laban sa Espanya, kung saan pinunit ng mga Katipunero ang kanilang mga cedula (residence certificate) bilang pagsuway sa kolonyal na pamahalaan.
	•	Bakit kontrobersyal?: Iba-iba ang accounts sa kung kailan at saan ito nangyari.
	•	Ano'ng nangyari?
	•	Dr. Pio Valenzuela: Nangyari ito sa Aug. 22–23, 1896, sa bahay ni Juan Ramos (anak ni Melchora Aquino).
	•	Mariano Alvarez: Aug. 23–24, 1896 sa Sampaukan, Bahay Toro, may higit 1000 Katipuneros.
	•	Gregoria de Jesus: Sinabi niyang Aug. 25 ang Cry at kinailangang tumakas siya mula sa mga sundalong Kastila.
	•	Guillermo Masangkay: Aug. 26 sa Balintawak, pinangunahan ni Andres Bonifacio.
	•	Bottom line: Bagamat hindi sigurado ang exact date and location, malinaw na ito ang hudyat ng rebolusyon laban sa Espanya.

Caused by the gravitational pull of the Moon (main influence) and the Sun on Earth's oceans. Results in high and low tides.

Caused by Earth’s rotation on its axis every 24 hours. One side of Earth faces the Sun (day), the other side is in shadow (night).

	•	• Caused by Earth’s revolution around the Sun (~365.25 days).

Caused by the tilt of Earth's axis and its orbit around the Sun.
• Summer Solstice
Occurs around June 21 (Northern Hemisphere); longest day of the year when the Sun is directly overhead at the Tropic of Cancer.
• Winter Solstice
Occurs around December 21 (Northern Hemisphere); shortest day of the year when the Sun is directly over the Tropic of Capricorn.
• Autumnal Equinox
Occurs around September 23; equal length of day and night (12 hours). The Sun is directly over the equator.
• Vernal Equinox
Occurs around March 21; also equal day and night. 
• Midnight Sun
Occurs in polar regions (e.g., Arctic Circle) during summer, where the Sun is visible for 24 hours due to the Earth's tilt.
Moon
Earth’s natural satellite; orbits Earth every 27.3 days. It reflects sunlight (does not emit its own light). Affects tides and lunar phases.
• Perigee
The Moon is at its closest point to Earth (~363,300 km); appears slightly larger (often called a supermoon).
• Apogee
The Moon is at its farthest point from Earth (~405,500 km); appears smaller (often called a micromoon).
Sun
A G-type main-sequence star; contains 99.8% of the solar system’s mass. Light takes 8 minutes to reach Earth, but thousands of years to escape the core.
• Perihelion
Earth is at its closest point to the Sun (~147 million km); occurs around early January.
• Aphelion
Earth is at its farthest point from the Sun (~152 million km); occurs around early July.

Layers of the Atmosphere (TrosMITE) 
Layer
Description
Trivia
Troposphere
- Lowest layer.
- Where weather, clouds, and life-supporting air exist.
- Altitude: 0–12 km.
Contains about 75% of the atmosphere's mass. Temperature decreases with altitude in this layer.
Stratosphere
- Contains the ozone layer, which absorbs harmful UV rays.
- Altitude: 12–50 km.
Commercial jets fly here to avoid turbulence. Temperature increases with altitude due to ozone absorption of sunlight.
Mesosphere
- Coldest layer.
- Meteors burn up here.
- Altitude: 50–85 km.
Hardest layer to study—too high for planes, too low for satellites. Temperatures can drop to -90°C.
Thermosphere
- Very hot layer (up to 2,000°C), but feels cold due to low particle density.
- Where auroras and space shuttles are found.
- Altitude: 85–600 km.
Despite high temperature, you'd feel cold here because particles are too sparse to transfer heat.
Ionosphere
- Overlaps Mesosphere and Thermosphere.
- Ionized by solar radiation.
- Crucial for radio and GPS signals.
Auroras (Northern and Southern Lights) occur here when solar particles collide with gas particles.
Exosphere
- Outermost layer; merges with outer space.
- Where satellites orbit.
- Altitude: 600–10,000 km.
Air is extremely thin—atoms can escape into space. This is technically where space begins.

Key Biological Concepts
	•	Autotrophs - Organisms that produce their own food using sunlight or chemicals.
	•	Heterotrophs - Organisms that obtain food by consuming other organisms.
	•	Prokaryotes - Simple cells, no nucleus, no membrane-bound organelles (e.g., bacteria).
	•	Eukaryotes - Complex cells, nucleus, membrane-bound organelles (e.g., plants, animals, fungi, protists).
2 Classifications of Cells (PUE-M)
	•	Prokaryotic - Single-celled organisms with no true nucleus.
	•	Example: Bacteria
	•	Eukaryotic - Organisms with a true nucleus.
	•	Example: Fungi, Animals, Plants, Protists

Asexual Reproductions (F-F-B-B-G-S-R-F-F-S)
	•	Fission - Asexual reproduction in which an organism splits into two or more individuals.
	•	Example: Bacteria
	•	Fragmentation - Asexual reproduction where a whole new individual grows from a fragment of the parent.
	•	Example: Starfish
	•	Budding - Asexual reproduction where a new organism develops from an outgrowth or bud due to cell division at one particular site.
	•	Example: Yeast
	•	Binary Fission - Asexual reproduction where a cell divides into two identical daughter cells.
	•	Example: Amoeba
	•	Gemmules - Internal buds formed by sponges for asexual reproduction.
	•	Spores - Reproductive cells produced by fungi, plants, and algae for asexual reproduction.
	•	Regeneration - The process of regrowing lost body parts or forming a whole new organism from a fragment.
	•	Example: Flatworms
	•	Vegetative Propagation - Asexual reproduction in plants using vegetative parts like stems, leaves, or roots.
	•	Example: Potatoes
	•	Fission (Plants) - A form of asexual reproduction in some plants where the plant divides into two or more parts, each capable of developing into a new plant.
Plant Hormones
	•	Auxins - Responsible for fruit ripening and root development.
	•	Cytokinins - Regulate cell division and cell differentiation.
	•	Gibberellins - Promote stem elongation, seed germination, and flowering.
	•	Abscisic Acid - Inhibits growth, promotes dormancy, and helps plants respond to stress.
	•	Ethylene - Promotes fruit ripening and senescence.

Animal vs. Plant Cell Differences
	•	Cell Walls - Present in plant cells, absent in animal cells. Provide structural support.
	•	Chloroplasts - Present in plant cells, absent in animal cells. Site of photosynthesis.

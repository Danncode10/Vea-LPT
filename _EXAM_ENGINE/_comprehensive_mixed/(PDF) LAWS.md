                                      PHILIPPINE CONSTITUTION

                                       Biak-na-Bato Constitution (1897)
       Established a Supreme Council, Council of Justice, and Assembly, modeled after Cuba’s system
       A provisional constitution adopted on November 1, 1897, but was never fully implemented due to
        the Pact of Biak-na-Bato in December

                                    First — Malolos Constitution (1899)
       Enacted on January 21, 1899, with 101 articles in 14 titles, influenced by Spanish and Latin American
        texts
       Declared popular sovereignty, civil liberties, separation of Church and State, and established a
        parliamentary-style republic

                                          Second — 1935 Constitution
       Created the Commonwealth of the Philippines, modeled on the U.S. Constitution, preparing for full
        independence
       Set up initially a unicameral legislature, later amended to bicameral Congress and extended voting
        rights, including women’s suffrage

                                        Third — 1943 Constitution
       Drafted under Japanese occupation; established a puppet republic headed by President Jose P.
        Laurel
       Created a strong executive, a unicameral legislature, and promoted Tagalog as the national
        language

                                        Fourth — 1973 Constitution
       Promulgated during the Marcos administration, shifted to a parliamentary system and gave Marcos
        expanded executive powers
       Imposed during Martial Law (from September 1972), which suspended key democratic rights

                                         Fifth — 1987 Constitution
       Drafted after the EDSA People Power Revolution, reinstated a presidential system and strengthened
        democratic safeguards
       Emphasizes sovereignty of the people, human rights, civil liberties, an independent Caribbean
        system, and includes 18 articles

                                                ARTICLE XIV
                       Education, Science and Technology, Arts, Culture, and Sports

Article XIV of the 1987 Philippine Constitution explains the responsibility of the State to provide quality
education, promote scientific and technological advancement, support artistic and cultural development,
and encourage sports programs. It ensures that these areas are protected and developed to benefit the people
and the country’s future.

                                         Article XIV – Key Highlights

 Section                                             Key Highlights

    1       State must ensure quality and accessible education at all levels.

    2       Provide free basic education, scholarships, loans, and support vocational/non-formal
            learning.

    3       Curriculum includes patriotism, ethics, human rights, science, skills, and optional religion.

    4       State regulates schools, requires Filipino ownership, and gives tax exemptions to nonprofits.

    5       Schools must meet regional needs, ensure academic freedom, equity, teacher support, and
            highest budget priority.

    6       Filipino is the national language; to be developed and used in instruction.

    7       Filipino and English are official languages; regional, Spanish, Arabic may be used.

    8       Constitution must be in Filipino and English with translations.
9     National Language Commission to develop and preserve languages.

10    State promotes research, innovation, and tech education.

11    Congress may give incentives for science and tech (e.g., scholarships, tax benefits).

12    State controls technology transfer and encourages local participation.

13    Protects intellectual property of scientists, inventors, and artists.

14    Support Filipino culture and creative freedom.

15    Promote arts, literature, and protect cultural heritage.

16    Cultural treasures protected and made accessible.

17    Recognize and protect indigenous rights and traditions.

18    Ensure equal access to cultural education and research.

19    Promote physical education, sports, and teamwork in schools.

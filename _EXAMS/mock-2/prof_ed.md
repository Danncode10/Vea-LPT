## RATIONALE (selected difficult items)

- **2 (D):** Conservation — understanding that quantity is unchanged despite a change in shape — is not yet mastered in Piaget's preoperational stage; the pupil is fooled by the taller column.
- **7 (B):** The gap between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with help is Vygotsky's zone of proximal development, the target zone for scaffolding.
- **17 (B):** Acting only to avoid getting caught is Stage 1 (punishment-avoidance) of Kohlberg's preconventional level — motivation, not the act itself, fixes the level.
- **20 (D):** Constructive alignment means objectives, activities, and assessment are deliberately matched; "spiral progression" and the "hidden curriculum" are distractors from K-12 vocabulary.
- **26 (D) vs 92 (D):** Item 26 is positive reinforcement (adding praise to increase a behavior); item 92 is negative reinforcement (removing an unpleasant outcome). Both increase behavior — the difference is add vs. remove, not reward vs. punishment.
- **32 (A) / 97 (B):** Logical handling of concrete objects = concrete operational; reasoning about hypotheticals = formal operational. The grade level alone is not the key — the cognitive behavior is.
- **36 (D) / 55 (D):** Validity = measures the right thing; reliability = measures consistently. A test can be reliable yet invalid, so read which property fails.
- **60 (A):** A near-universal correct answer means the item is easy (high facility / low difficulty); it does not automatically tell you about discrimination or validity — the "always" options overstate.
- **80 (D):** "Design a solution" is creating — the top of Bloom's revised taxonomy — not merely applying; the verb signals the level.
- **92 (D):** Removing the threat of losing recess to raise work completion is negative reinforcement, frequently mistaken for punishment.
- **118 (C) / 84 (A):** Both expose construct misalignment: testing a lower cognitive level than the objective demands defeats the objective even if the items are otherwise fine.
- **126 (B):** Judging pupils against fixed mastery standards is criterion-referenced; ranking them against each other would be norm-referenced.
- **132 (D):** Intermittent (partial) reinforcement produces behavior that resists extinction more than continuous reinforcement — the counter-intuitive but classic finding.
- **146 (D):** A task that is neither too easy nor too hard sits in the zone of proximal development — the same construct as item 7, applied to task design.
